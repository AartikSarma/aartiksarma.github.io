Pulmonary & critical care · UCSF

Aartik Sarma

I take care of people in the intensive care unit, and I study why two patients with the same diagnosis can respond so differently to the same treatment.

Critical illness is defined by what we can see at the bedside — a low oxygen level, a chest X-ray, a blood pressure. Those definitions gather together patients whose underlying biology has almost nothing in common, which is one reason so many promising treatments work in the lab and then fail in large trials. My lab reads the molecular state of the lungs and blood directly, looking for the distinctions our current definitions miss.

What I do

01

Care for patients

I work as an attending physician in the ICU, caring for people with respiratory failure, sepsis, and other critical illness. Every research question I have started with a patient in front of me.

02

Read the lung directly

We sequence RNA from airway fluid and blood in critically ill patients — a molecular read-out of what the immune system is actually doing, which no bedside measurement can show.

03

Build the tools

Analysis of this kind is only as trustworthy as the code behind it, so I write and release it — statistical tools, federated ICU data pipelines, and the analysis for each of my papers.
